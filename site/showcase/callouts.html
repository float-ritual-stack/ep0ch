Feature

Callouts

A boxed aside in a note. Write > [!warning] Title, as Obsidian does, and the door draws it in a frame with its icon. Callouts nest, fold, and change type with a click. Use one when a line has to stand apart from the prose: a risk, a summary, a question waiting on someone.

Try it

  1. Open the kitchen sink. It runs on its own outline of made-up notes, so nothing you do reaches yours.

    shellbash
    ep0ch --showcase

    the index of door parts on the left, and section 1 beside it.

  2. Click callouts at the foot of the index, or press j until it's selected. Then press ⏎ to go into it.

    two readers: Obsidian's own examples on the left, and a type this outline declares on the right.

  3. Press ] until the first callout's ✎ icon is current, then ⏎. Press j and ⏎.

    the type choice open under the frame, then the callout redrawn as ≡ abstract.

  4. Press ctrlz. Then click the title of the nested ⚠ warning.

    the type put back, then the warning folded to one line. Folding never changes the note's text.

Write one

A callout is a block quote whose first line names a type in [! ]. Everything quoted after it is its body. Quote one level deeper to put a callout inside another.

note · Allotment gatemarkdown
Allotment gate [place::gate]

> [!warning] The gate sticks
> Lift it as you pull.
> > [!tip]- Why
> > The hinge post leans. It is on the jobs list.

✎ The type choice is yoursnote

An agent can't open the type choice in your reader (callout.menu refuses it). It changes a callout with callout.type or callout.start, checked against the revision it read.

Reference

callout.type n=<number>? line=<number>? to=<string>

Changes a callout's type: its header's [!type] is rewritten through the note's save, checked against the revision read now, recorded as whoever asks and said on screen.
