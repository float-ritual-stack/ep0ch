# A cold frame, from the allotment society's leaflet

A cold frame is a bottomless box with a glass lid, set on the soil. It is a step between the windowsill and the open bed.

Seedlings grown indoors are soft. Moved straight outside they scorch in the sun and sulk in the wind. Hardening off is the week or two in the frame that toughens them: the lid open a crack on mild days, wider each morning, shut at dusk.

Watch the thermometer, not the calendar. A frame left shut on a bright March afternoon can pass thirty degrees by two o'clock, and cook what it was meant to shelter.

Close the lid tonight if frost is forecast. Prop it open in the morning before you go to work.
